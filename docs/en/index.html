The local novel studio

A quiet desk,
a sharp needle.

Versorium is a writing studio for novelists that runs on your own computer: every chapter is a Markdown file in a folder you own, every change is kept in its history, and the AI is the one you choose, or none.

Why

What makes it different

It comes down to four decisions. Each has a reason, and some still come with a limit worth knowing about.

On your machine

Your novel lives on your disk

A novel takes years. In that time a service can close, change its terms or lose your account; a folder on your disk is still there. Versorium keeps each novel as an ordinary folder, works offline and carries no analytics or telemetry.

On its own, it does one thing over the network: it checks GitHub for updates at launch, with nothing from your novel, and you can turn that off in Settings → Application. Everything else happens when you ask for it: downloading a model, sending the novel to GitHub or bringing it back, or rewriting with a command-line tool, which sends the passage to its own service.

Changes are saved on their own, atomically, 800 ms after you stop typing. Back up now puts the whole novel, history included, in one zip and leaves it in up to three places: iCloud Drive, Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, Nextcloud, ownCloud or a second disk. Restore extracts it beside the novel, never over it.

Today Backups run when you press the button; nothing is scheduled.

History

The story, keystroke by keystroke

Every change in the editor is written down as an operation (what was typed or deleted, where, and by whom: you or an AI) in an append-only log kept inside the novel. Every 200 operations the whole chapter is saved as well.

On top of that, every novel is a git repository. Versorium takes a snapshot every minute when something changed, before an AI writes and before a chapter is deleted, and you can save your own with a description.

Restore (Cmd/Ctrl+Alt+R) puts back the previous text of the selection, or of the word under the cursor.

Today Restore reaches back through the current session, up to 1000 edits. Snapshots and the log on disk cannot be browsed or restored from inside the app; going back to a snapshot is done with git, outside it. Assistants connected through MCP can read the log.

AI

The AI you choose, or none

Versorium ships with no AI switched on. If you want one, you choose where it comes from: a model that runs inside the app with llama.cpp, Ollama if you already use it, or the tools already in your terminal: Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode.

For the built-in AI there is a catalogue of 11 writing models in four sizes, from 0.58 to 17.4 GB. The app recommends the largest size that fits your machine with room to spare, downloads only when you press Download, resumes what was cut off and checks every file against SHA-256.

To rewrite, you select a passage and see the difference before applying it; Versorium takes a snapshot first and records the change under the AI's name. Each provider is labelled Local or CLI, because a CLI tool sends the passage to its own service.

Assistants connected through MCP (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex and OpenCode set up in one click; Cursor and VS Code by hand) can only read, unless you grant write access to one in particular. Even then, every write first returns the diff and is applied only by a second call that confirms it, after a snapshot.

Today The AI does two jobs: rewriting, and checking continuity, which reasons over chapter titles and codex excerpts, not over the prose. Chat, search by meaning and dictation do not exist yet. The built-in AI runs inside the app, so if its engine crashes, Versorium closes with it; autosave and snapshots limit what is lost.

Files

Files that open without Versorium

Every chapter is a .md file with a YAML header. The header belongs to the app (title, status, word count) and the rest is your text, in no proprietary format. Renaming a chapter changes its title, not its file; the order lives in versorium.json.

You do not need to install git: it is built into the app. If you already use it, the folder is an ordinary repository.

It exports Markdown, DOCX in standard manuscript format, EPUB 3 (it passes epubcheck 5.2.1 with no errors or warnings), PDF and a Scrivener 3 project. DOCX, EPUB and PDF get a typographic title page and a colophon, and you can turn off either one per project. It imports Markdown, DOCX, EPUB and Scrivener projects, and shows you a preview, with its warnings, before writing anything.

You can send the novel to a GitHub repository and bring it back. The token lives in your system's credential store and only ever travels to github.com.

Today The GitHub repository and its remote are set up outside the app, and bringing changes back only works when the two histories have not diverged: it does not merge. DOCX and Scrivener drop scene titles, and say so; the PDF prints them, except on a chapter's first scene, where the title is lost without a warning. The PDF is US Letter, set in Times-Roman, and replaces emoji and non-Latin scripts, with a warning.

On the desk

What is on the desk

A few tools, made for spending long hours with a manuscript.

The editor
Markdown in CodeMirror, the word count in the status bar, and Cmd/Ctrl+F to find text in the chapter.
Focus and typewriter
Focus dims everything but the page, and Escape always gets you out. Typewriter keeps the line you are writing in one place on screen.
Binder
Chapters can be created, renamed, moved up or down, and marked draft, revised or final. Deleting a chapter takes a snapshot first; deleting a novel sends it to the system trash.
Templates
Blank, Three acts, Save the Cat and Kishōtenketsu, offered in the first-run guide, which you can replay from Settings → Application. They only add chapter titles, never prose, in the language of the interface.
Title page and authorship
A typographic title page and a colophon in DOCX, EPUB and PDF. Two author profiles, work and personal, whose details reach the metadata of every format but Scrivener.
English and Spanish
The whole interface in both languages. It starts in your system's language and switches whenever you like.
No account
No sign-up and no account. The first time, five steps you can skip; nothing is created until you confirm the project.
Crash reports without prose
Kept on your machine with no paths, email addresses or tokens, and no prose: any run of six or more words is removed. They leave only if you press Report, which opens a GitHub issue in your browser.
Credentials in the keychain
Tokens go to your system's credential store (Keychain, Credential Manager or Secret Service), never to a settings file.

Not yet

What it does not do yet

Better to know before you download.

  • Talking to your novel: there is a slot for a chat model in Settings, but no chat window.
  • Search by meaning. The app's search is plain text, within the chapter.
  • Dictation: there is no speech model.
  • Continuing a chapter with AI (Creative mode): the Continue chapter button is visible but disabled.
  • Going back to an older snapshot, or recovering a deleted chapter, from inside the app. Today that is done with git.
  • Browsing the change log on disk from inside the app.
  • Creating the GitHub repository or adding the remote from inside the app.
  • Scheduled backups.
  • Generating with a local OpenAI-compatible server such as LM Studio or llama-server: for now it only tests the connection.
  • Reordering chapters by dragging, image covers and downloadable fonts.
  • Dedicated editors for the codex, the plot or research notes: the folders exist, but they are filled outside the app.
  • Apple and Windows code signing: see The first time you open it, below.

Download

For macOS, Windows and Linux

Versorium is free. No release has been published yet, and no installer has been built. The installers will be on the GitHub releases page when the first release is out, and the links below already point there. So far, the app has only been run on macOS.

macOS

The first release will have two .dmg downloads: one for Apple silicon and one for Intel.

The app has run on an Apple silicon Mac (an M4 Max), where the built-in AI uses Metal: the first run takes about 15 s to compile, and the app does that ahead of time at launch. It has not been tried on an Intel Mac.

Releases page (macOS)

Windows

The first release will have x64 installers in two formats, MSI and NSIS.

The app has not been run on Windows yet. The built-in AI will use Vulkan (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel); there will be no CUDA, on purpose. It will need an x86-64-v2 processor (SSE4.2).

Releases page (Windows)

Linux

The first release will have x64 .deb, .rpm and AppImage packages, built on Ubuntu 22.04 (webkit2gtk-4.1).

The app has not been run on Linux yet: only the automated tests run there, and the built-in AI is compiled with Vulkan, but no real GPU has used it. The .deb will declare libvulkan1 and libssl3. It will need an x86-64-v2 processor (SSE4.2). Saving a token will need Secret Service; without it, the app will say so.

The first time you open it

macOS

macOS will say the app is damaged. It is not.

The bundle will carry no Developer ID signature, so macOS will quarantine it and Gatekeeper will report the most misleading message it has. Clear the quarantine flag once, in Terminal:

xattr -rd com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Versorium.app

Run it only on a build you took from the releases page — that flag is the check that protects you from a tampered download.

Windows

SmartScreen will interrupt the installer.

The installer will be unsigned, for the same reason as the macOS bundle, so Windows will show the blue “Windows protected your PC” screen. Choose More info, then Run anyway. Signed builds need Apple and Microsoft certificates, which do not exist yet, plus changes to the release workflow.

Updates are signed with minisign: the app installs one only if its signature matches the public key built into it and its SHA-256 matches the release's SHA256SUMS. It checks for them once at launch, with no account and no token; you can turn that off in Settings → Application. A GitHub token is optional: it is only needed to read a private repository, or to get past GitHub's limit of 60 requests an hour without one. With no release out yet, this path has not been tried end to end.

Questions

Worth asking first

Does my novel leave my computer?

No, except for what you ask for. The novel is a folder on your disk, and Versorium carries no analytics or telemetry.

On its own, the app does one thing over the network: it checks GitHub for updates at launch, with nothing from your novel, and you can turn that off in Settings → Application. Everything else happens when you ask for it: downloading a model from Hugging Face, sending the novel to GitHub or bringing it back, or rewriting with Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode, which send the passage to their own service; that is why the Rewrite dialog labels every provider Local or CLI. If you choose a synced folder such as iCloud Drive or Dropbox as a backup destination, the app tells you that copy leaves the machine. Crash reports are kept on your machine, without prose, and leave only if you press Report.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up and no Versorium account, and the app checks for updates without asking you for anything.

A GitHub token is needed only if you choose to send the novel to a GitHub repository and bring it back; for updates it is optional. It is kept in your system's credential store, never in a file.

Do I need git installed?

No. Versorium has git built in (libgit2): every novel is a repository from the moment you create it, with nothing to install. If you already use git, you can open the folder with your usual tools.

Which AI does it use?

None, until you choose one. You can download a model from its catalogue to run inside the app with llama.cpp, use Ollama, or hand the work to Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode.

Today the AI rewrites passages, showing you the difference first, and checks continuity from the chapter titles and the codex. Chat, search by meaning and dictation do not exist yet.

What does “free and open source” mean here?

That the code is published under the Apache-2.0 licence: you can use it, study it, change it and redistribute it, commercially too, as long as you keep the licence notices. The app costs nothing and asks for no account.

The Versorium name and mark are not part of the licence: a fork has to change them before it is distributed.

Can I take my novel to another program?

Yes. The chapters are already Markdown files that any editor opens, and the app exports Markdown, DOCX, EPUB 3, PDF and Scrivener 3 projects.

To start from another program, it imports Markdown, DOCX, EPUB and Scrivener projects; DOCX, EPUB and Scrivener lose bold, italics and scene structure.
